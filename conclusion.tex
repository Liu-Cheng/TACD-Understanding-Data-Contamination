\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a systematic study of data contamination detection in Verilog code generation benchmarks. We first evaluated 10 existing probes on real benchmarks and found that they frequently disagree, demonstrating that no single probe is reliable for contamination detection. To address this, we constructed golden datasets for three DeepSeek-Coder-based models and proposed a CatBoost ensemble that fuses five selected probes: DC-PDD, Reference, Neighbor, Min-K\%, and Zlib. On the same-architecture test sets, our ensemble achieves an average AUC of 0.927 and F1 of 0.872, significantly outperforming all individual probes. On three held-out models with distinct architectures (CodeLlama, CodeQwen, and Qwen), it maintains strong performance with an average AUC of 0.880 and F1 of 0.792. Applying the detector to real-world benchmarks — VerilogEval, RTLLM, and RealBench — revealed an overall weighted average contamination rate of 52.0\%. Among all models, RTLCoder exhibits the highest contamination rate at 84.4\%, while VerilogEval v1 shows the highest contamination rate among all benchmarks at 65.4\%. These findings highlight that data contamination is a systemic problem in current Verilog benchmarks. Future work includes exploring mitigation strategies for partially contaminated benchmarks and extending detection to semantically equivalent but syntactically diverse variants.
