\begin{abstract}

Large language models (LLMs) for Verilog generation frequently report functional-correctness rates above 95\% on widely used benchmarks. However, such results do not consistently translate into effectiveness on practical RTL designs, making it difficult for current evaluations to faithfully measure and guide progress in Verilog generation. Data contamination is a critical concern in this setting. Publicly available hardware-design data are scarce, and many RTL benchmarks aggregate examples from public Internet sources. Because LLM pretraining and fine-tuning data are often collected from the same limited data ecosystem, training and evaluation sets have a high risk of overlap, potentially inflating reported performance.
We systematically evaluate ten representative detectors and find that they yield highly inconsistent contamination estimates for the same model--benchmark pairs. To improve reliability, we construct controlled datasets with known membership labels and develop a boosting-based detector that integrates complementary signals from existing methods, achieving an average AUC of 0.9208 and outperforming individual detectors. We then apply the detector to representative Verilog-generation LLMs and standard RTL benchmarks, revealing substantial estimated contamination rates of approximately 40\%--70\%. These results suggest that current benchmark scores may overestimate model generalization and underscore the need for contamination-aware evaluation protocols that more accurately guide the development of Verilog-generation LLMs.

\end{abstract}
